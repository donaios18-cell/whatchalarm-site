How it works

1

Set it on the watch, before bed

Pick your latest wake-up time and a window — 10 to 30 minutes — and tap Start.

2

Sleep. Your phone can be anywhere

The alarm lives on the watch. The iPhone can be in another room, face down, or switched off entirely.

3

It watches for the shallow end

When the window opens, the watch reads wrist movement and heart rate. Turning over, a rising pulse — the signals that you are already close to the surface.

4

Gentle taps first

It starts with light taps on the wrist rather than a jolt, and escalates only if you stay asleep.

What's different about it

It runs on the watch, not the phone

Most smart alarms lean on the iPhone doing work in the background — which iOS grants sparingly, and which stops entirely if you keep Background App Refresh switched off. WhatchAlarm asks the watch for its own wake-up window, so nothing depends on what your phone is allowed to do overnight.

The deadline is never negotiable

The wake-up at your latest time is scheduled the moment you tap Start. Even if the app is shut down, the battery gets low, or the detection finds nothing, that alarm is already in the queue.

Nothing leaves your wrist

No account, no sign-in, no servers, no analytics. Your heart rate is read on the watch, used on the watch, and never transmitted anywhere — not to the developer, not to anyone.

Honest about what it is

Sleep-phase detection from a wrist sensor is a good heuristic, not a laboratory measurement. WhatchAlarm treats it that way: it only ever moves your alarm earlier, inside a window you chose.

What you need

  • Apple Watch running watchOS 10 or later
  • A paired iPhone running iOS 17 or later
  • Permission to read heart rate, granted on the watch
  • WhatchAlarm allowed through Sleep Focus, so the alarm isn't muted
